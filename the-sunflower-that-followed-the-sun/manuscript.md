## Scene 1: A Tall New Neighbor

Zara and her father planted sunflower seeds beside the garden fence. Weeks later, one plant rose higher than Zara’s shoulders. Its broad green leaves looked like hands reaching into the morning air. At the top was a round flower bud, still closed tight. Zara named it Sunny. Every day she checked how much taller it had grown. ‘Will it be taller than you?’ she asked Dad. ‘Perhaps,’ he said. ‘Let us measure it and see.’ They tied a soft ribbon to a measuring stick, not to the growing stem.

## Scene 2: Facing Morning

One morning, Zara noticed that Sunny’s bud pointed toward the bright eastern sky. She drew an arrow in her notebook. At lunchtime, she returned. The bud seemed to face a different direction. Zara blinked. ‘Did you turn?’ she asked. The plant did not answer. Dad explained that young sunflowers can slowly change the direction of their growing heads during the day. Zara imagined Sunny following the light across the sky. She decided to watch carefully instead of guessing.

## Scene 3: The Shadow Game

Dad helped Zara mark the shadow of a garden stick on the ground in the morning. They marked it again at midday and late afternoon. The shadow changed place and length. ‘The sun looks as if it moves across our sky,’ Dad said. ‘That helps us understand why the light comes from different directions.’ Zara compared her shadow marks with her sunflower drawings. She saw that the garden could tell a story with light, even when nobody spoke.

**Illustration brief:** Zara and Dad mark three shadow positions from a vertical garden stick; sunlit garden.

## Scene 4: A Cloudy Surprise

The next day was cloudy. Zara hurried outside, hoping to see Sunny turn again. The sky was pale and the sunlight was weak. She watched for a while but could not tell whether the bud was moving. ‘Maybe Sunny is confused,’ she said. Dad reminded her that plants respond to many things and that changes can be hard to notice. ‘One cloudy day does not tell us everything,’ he said. Zara wrote CLOUDY in her notebook and left room for tomorrow’s observations.

## Scene 5: A Golden Face

After more days of growth, Sunny’s bud began to open. Bright yellow petals spread around a dark center. Zara clapped her hands. ‘You have a face now!’ she said. She noticed something else: the open flower seemed to keep facing roughly toward the morning sun. Dad explained that mature sunflower heads usually stop making the same daily turning movement that young growing buds can show. Zara looked at her old drawings. Sunny had changed in more ways than one.

**Illustration brief:** Open mature sunflower with golden petals facing eastward morning light; Zara comparing notebook sketches.

## Scene 6: Visitors to Sunny

Soon bees began visiting Sunny. Zara watched from the path, keeping her hands to herself. A bee landed on the flower and moved across its center. Another came a moment later. Dad told Zara that visiting insects can help many flowers make seeds by moving pollen. Zara felt proud of the little garden she had helped plant. Sunny was not just tall and bright. It was part of a busy place where plants and animals depended on one another.

## Scene 7: The Sunflower Diary

At the end of the week, Zara spread her drawings across the table. There was the tiny plant, the turning bud, the cloudy day, the golden flower, and the visiting bees. ‘I thought the flower followed the sun forever,’ she said. ‘Now I know young buds and open flowers behave differently.’ Dad smiled. Zara placed the pages into a folder marked SUNNY’S DIARY. Tomorrow she would look for new changes, but she already knew something wonderful: careful watching could turn a simple question into a discovery.

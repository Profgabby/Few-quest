# Tobi and the Tiny Garden Visitor

## Scene 1: Something on the Leaf

Tobi was helping Auntie Nneka check the bean plants. He lifted one broad leaf and spotted a tiny green creature resting underneath. It had six little legs and two long feelers. ‘Auntie! There is someone here,’ he whispered. Auntie Nneka leaned closer without touching the leaf. ‘A small insect,’ she said. ‘Let us watch before we decide what it is doing.’ Tobi had never thought of a garden leaf as a place where another creature might rest.

## Scene 2: The Tiny Trail

The little insect walked slowly along the leaf’s edge. Tobi followed it with his eyes. A second insect sat near the stem. Tobi noticed a few curled leaves on the same plant. ‘Did the insects do that?’ he asked. Auntie Nneka said it was possible, but they needed to look closely. They checked more leaves and found several tiny insects together. Tobi drew what he saw: a leaf, a stem, and small green dots. He did not give his drawing a name yet.

**Illustration brief:** Cluster of small green aphid-like insects near bean stem, some curled leaves; observation notebook.

## Scene 3: A Red Dot Arrives

A bright red beetle landed nearby. It had black spots and a shiny rounded back. ‘Look at that one!’ Tobi said. The beetle walked toward the cluster of green insects. Auntie Nneka told him it was a ladybird beetle. Many ladybird beetles eat aphids, small insects that feed on plant sap. Tobi watched the red beetle pause and then move among the green insects. ‘So the garden has hunters too,’ he whispered. Auntie Nneka nodded. ‘And every visitor has a part in the story.’

## Scene 4: Look, Don’t Grab

Tobi reached toward the beetle, but Auntie Nneka gently stopped him. ‘Let us leave it where it is,’ she said. ‘Small creatures can be hurt by rough hands.’ Tobi pulled his fingers back. They watched from the path instead. The beetle moved beneath another leaf and disappeared from sight. Tobi was disappointed for a moment. Then he remembered the curled leaves, the green insects, and the red beetle. There was still plenty to discover without catching anything.

**Illustration brief:** Tobi withdraws hand as aunt guides him to observe insects without touching.

## Scene 5: A Garden Count

The next morning, Tobi brought his notebook. Auntie Nneka helped him choose three bean leaves to observe. They counted the small green insects they could see on each leaf and wrote the numbers down. They also looked for ladybird beetles. On one leaf, Tobi saw none. On another, he found a single red beetle. ‘The garden is different every day,’ he said. Auntie Nneka agreed. ‘That is why careful notes are useful.’ They left the leaves attached to the plants.

## Scene 6: The Leaf with Holes

While they worked, Tobi noticed holes in a different leaf. ‘More aphids?’ he asked. Auntie Nneka shook her head. ‘Not necessarily. Many things can make holes in leaves. Aphids usually feed by sucking sap.’ They looked for chewing insects but did not find one that morning. Tobi added a question mark to his drawing. He liked that he could write down a question without pretending to know the answer. The garden was teaching him how to be curious.

**Illustration brief:** Bean leaf with small chewing holes, Tobi drawing question mark; no false insect identification.

## Scene 7: A Home for Many

At the end of the week, Tobi stood by the bean bed and looked carefully at the leaves. He could see the green insects again, and a ladybird beetle rested near the top of the plant. He had not solved every mystery. He had learned something better: a garden was home to many living things. ‘I will keep watching,’ he told Auntie Nneka. She handed him his notebook. ‘And I will keep asking good questions,’ he added, smiling.

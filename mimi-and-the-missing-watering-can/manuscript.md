# Mimi and the Missing Watering Can

**Collection:** GardenVerse · **Tier:** Little Explorers · **Language:** English
**Editorial status:** Draft for review

## Scene 1: The Empty Shelf

Mimi came to the garden with a plan. She would water the bean seedlings before the sun grew hot. But the blue watering can was missing from its usual shelf. Mimi looked above the shelf, below it, and behind a basket of gloves. Nothing. ‘That is strange,’ she said. She had washed the can yesterday and left it to dry. Her little brother, Kele, appeared at the gate holding a paper windmill. ‘What are you looking for?’ he asked. ‘My blue watering can,’ Mimi said. ‘It has vanished!’

## Scene 2: A Trail of Drops

Kele pointed at three dark spots on the stone path. ‘Maybe those are clues.’ Mimi knelt to look. The spots were wet, but they might have come from someone’s shoes. She followed them toward the bean bed. There she found a small puddle beside the tap. ‘Perhaps someone filled the can,’ she said. Kele shook his head. ‘Or perhaps the tap dripped.’ Mimi touched the tap handle. It was closed, but one last drop fell from its spout. They needed a better clue.

**Illustration brief:** Children examine wet stones near outdoor tap; no misleading clear trail.

## Scene 3: The Wrong Blue Thing

Behind the tomato pots, Mimi saw something bright blue. ‘Found it!’ she cried. But when she stepped closer, it was only Kele’s toy bucket. Kele picked it up. ‘I could carry water in this,’ he offered. Mimi laughed. ‘You could, but it would take many trips.’ They searched the tool shed next. A rake leaned against the wall. A coil of hose hung on a hook. There was no watering can. Mimi began to feel cross, then remembered that Kele was trying to help.

## Scene 4: A Voice by the Fence

At the fence, they heard a soft voice singing. Their neighbor, Auntie Sade, was tending a row of young lettuce plants on the other side. ‘Good morning!’ Mimi called. ‘Have you seen our watering can?’ Auntie Sade stopped singing. ‘A blue one with a white flower painted on it?’ Mimi nodded. ‘I saw your grandmother carrying it this morning,’ Auntie Sade said. ‘She went toward the new flower bed.’ Mimi thanked her. At last, they had a clue from someone who had actually seen the can.

## Scene 5: The New Flower Bed

Mimi and Kele hurried toward the new flower bed, but they slowed when they reached the narrow path. Grandma was kneeling beside a tray of marigold seedlings. The blue watering can stood behind a large pot, almost hidden from view. ‘There it is!’ Mimi said. Grandma looked up. ‘Oh! I borrowed it to settle these seedlings into the soil. I meant to put it back.’ Mimi felt relief wash over her. The can had not vanished at all. It had simply been borrowed without a message.

## Scene 6: A Better Plan

Grandma apologized for leaving Mimi to search. Mimi admitted that she had almost blamed Kele when she could not find the can. ‘I am glad you asked questions instead,’ Grandma said. Kele suggested making a small sign for the shelf. On one side it would say HERE. On the other, IN USE. Mimi liked the idea. They painted the sign together, then took turns watering the beans and marigolds. Nobody had to guess where the watering can had gone.

## Scene 7: The Can Comes Home

When the watering was finished, Mimi emptied the little can and placed it upside down where it could dry. She turned the sign to HERE. Kele stood back to admire their work. ‘Next time, we can look at the sign first,’ he said. ‘And ask before we worry,’ Mimi added. Grandma nodded. The beans stood fresh in their bed, and the marigolds had their first drink. Mimi picked up her notebook. There was one last thing to write: Mystery solved—with help from everyone.
